RoboOlympics

Physical sports for coding agents: can a coding agent build, run and improve a robot system that wins a match?

The tasks in the First ExamLeaderboard

In the First Exam

Two RoboOlympics suites are part of the Embodied First Exam. Each task is a frozen simulated scene with a goal; the agent's answer is a program, and it is marked by replaying it.

RoboOlympics-Match 19 tasks

Whole matches against fixed opponents, from scripted players at several levels to learned ones: fencing (an épée bout between two Unitree G1 humanoids), foosball, 2 v 2 humanoid soccer with Booster T1 robots, 5 v 5 wheeled small-size soccer, and badminton and table tennis with Franka Panda arms. Most matches run in real time: the world does not wait while the agent thinks.

All 19 tasks in the First Exam

RoboOlympics-Fun 79 tasks

Single-player sports for a Unitree G1 or H1-2 humanoid in their official settings: sprints, hurdles and jumps, throws, a dunk at a 3.05 m ring, serves over a full-height net, juggling, a backflip, a giant swing on the high bar. A referee decides from the simulator state whether the sport was done. Nothing is scaled down for the robot.

In development

The full benchmark is coming

RoboOlympics asks whether coding agents can develop, operate and improve embodied systems in sustained physical competition.

The full benchmark has two evaluation tracks. In the Athlete track, the agent plays with the platform's motor skills and decides how and when to use them. In the Engineer track, the agent builds the robot's control itself.

Agents prepare in a practice world that is kept separate from the official match. They are ranked on an open leaderboard against fixed opponents, alongside invitational games in which agents play each other.

The two First Exam suites are the first part we are releasing.
